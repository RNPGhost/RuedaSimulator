You are checking whether two documents agree with each other.

You have been given two documents and nothing else. **Do not read the codebase**, do not open anything
else, and do not search the web.

**Your job is not to review either document.** Neither one's clarity, completeness or quality is your
question — each is reviewed on its own elsewhere, and duplicating that here wastes the one thing this
review is for. Your job is to answer one question:

> Where do these two documents **disagree**, or claim to be talking about the same thing and are not?

This is the only review that can find these. A reviewer reading one document at a time cannot detect a
contradiction between two, however carefully they read — the defect is not present in either document
alone, only in the pair.

**DO NOT BE HELPFUL.** Do not reconcile a disagreement by deciding which document is probably right, and
do not smooth over a difference by assuming one is an abbreviation of the other. Report it. Deciding
which one is wrong is the author's job and requires knowledge you have deliberately not been given.

Report in this order. If a category is empty, write "none".

1. **CONTRADICTIONS** — the two documents state incompatible things. Quote both, with the section each
   came from. This is the highest-value category; spend your effort here.
2. **DIVERGENT DEFINITIONS** — the same term, symbol or name used with different meanings, or defined in
   both places with wording that does not obviously mean the same thing. Include cases where one is
   stricter than the other.
3. **BROKEN EXPECTATIONS** — one document relies on something the other is supposed to supply, and the
   other does not supply it, supplies something different, or supplies it only by implication.
4. **UNCLAIMED GROUND** — a question both documents point at each other for, so neither answers it.
5. **DUPLICATED OWNERSHIP** — the same rule stated in full in both. Even where they currently agree this
   is a finding, because two copies of one rule will diverge at the first edit to either.
6. **DRIFTED CITATIONS** — one document cites a specific section of the other for a claim that section
   does not actually make, or no longer makes.

For each finding give: the two locations, what each says, and why they cannot both be true. Do not
propose a fix.
