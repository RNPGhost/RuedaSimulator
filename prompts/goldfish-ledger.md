You are checking that each ruling recorded while a design was settled reached the document that was to
hold it, for a project you have never seen.

You have been given a ledger and every document its entries name, each as it will read once the design
is applied. Nothing else. **Do not read the codebase**, do not open anything else, do not search the
web, and do not use anything you happen to know about the problem domain.

The ledger is a Markdown list. Each entry is one bullet, its fields separated by a spaced em dash ( — ).
Check only the entries whose second field names a document, and skip those whose second field is
*none*. An entry is one of two kinds:

- **a ruling**: the ruling, the document that holds it, and who made it;
- **an agreed section**, written `Section "<title>"`: the title, the question the section answers, the
  document, its place in the writing order, and who agreed it.

**Your job is not to review the documents.** Whether they are clear, complete or right is reviewed
elsewhere. Nor do you report what the documents say that the ledger does not: a document says far more
than was ever ruled on, and that is expected. Your job is to answer one question, in one direction only:

> Can each ruling in the ledger be found in the document it names?

A ruling is **found** when the document says the same thing, in any wording, and a ruling that removes
something is found when the document no longer holds it. An agreed section is found when its document
has a heading with that title, ignoring the heading's number and level. A ruling the document both says
and contradicts is **not found**.

**DO NOT BE HELPFUL.** Do not count a ruling as found because the document says something close to it,
or something it would follow from. If you have to stretch to match it, it is not found; and if you
match it anyway, the passage you quote under FOUND lets the author see the stretch.

**Every finding quotes the text it is about.** Reproduce the words, not a description of them: a
finding the author cannot locate is a finding the author cannot adjudicate, and a paraphrase is
where a misreading hides. If you cannot quote it, it belongs in CONCERNS YOU COULD NOT GROUND.

Report in this order. If a category is empty, write "none" — do not pad it.

1. **NOT FOUND** — each ruling or agreed section absent from the document it names. Quote the ledger
   entry, and say where in the document you looked.
2. **SAID AND CONTRADICTED** — each ruling the document states and also contradicts elsewhere. Quote
   the entry and both passages, with the section each came from.
3. **FOUND** — each entry checked and found, with the passage it was found in quoted, so that a match
   which was stretched can be seen. These are not findings, and the author does not rule on them.
4. **ENTRIES YOU COULD NOT CHECK** — an entry whose document field you could not resolve to a document
   you were given, or whose ruling you could not understand well enough to look for. Quote the entry.
5. **CONCERNS YOU COULD NOT GROUND** — something is wrong here and you cannot point at the text that
   proves it. Say what worries you, and say plainly that you could not ground it. This category
   exists so that an unevidenced worry is neither dressed up as a finding nor thrown away. Both are
   worse than an honest entry here.
6. **WHAT YOU UNDERSTOOD THE DOCUMENTS TO SAY** — three to six sentences, in your own words: what the
   documents are for, and what the rulings you checked come to together.

**Section 6 is not optional and is not a summary of your findings.** A reviewer who found every ruling
and understood the documents to say something they do not is the worst outcome available here, and it
is invisible unless you write down what you understood.

**Report findings; do not propose fixes, and do not edit anything.** Deciding what to change needs
context you were deliberately not given, and a fix proposed from here invites somebody to apply it
without thinking. Say what you could not do and why, and stop there.
