# Section review — ELEPHANT.md §3, opening and *The four points in the process*

Scope: passage 1 (the §3 heading through the paragraph on the two skills) and passage 2 (*The four
points in the process*, through the abandonment paragraph). The rest of `ELEPHANT.md` and the whole of
`METHOD.md` were read only to resolve what these passages rely on. The copy of `ELEPHANT.md` I was given
ends at §7, so it has no §8, §9 or §10.

## 1. BLOCKING

**B1. What loads the `elephant` skill for the proposal.**
Passage 1: *"The `elephant` skill runs the proposal (§1), steps 1 to 7, and abandoning an elephant. The
`close-out` skill runs step 8, and is loaded when the author says the design is finished or asks to
sign it off. They are two, not one, because a skill is loaded when a request matches its description"*.
The passage gives a trigger for `close-out` and none for `elephant`. By its own premise, a skill loads
only when a request matches its description. But the proposal is owed *"At the start of any change that
would produce new or changed intent, a new document about the work, or a change to what an existing one
requires"* (§1). Those requests can be about any subject, and none of them needs to mention a design
conversation. So I cannot write the `elephant` skill's description, because I do not know what request
it is supposed to match at proposal time. It could be:
(a) the skill's description is written broadly enough to match the start of any change. Nothing
says how, and this runs against the passage's own reason for having two skills;
(b) `METHOD.md`'s elephant section carries the duty to propose (§1 quotes it: *"proposing it is not
[optional]"*), and the skill loads only after the author opts in. On this reading the skill does not
"run the proposal" at all.
In the absence of an answer I would have assumed (b) and treated "runs the proposal" as loose wording.
That is a guess, and it changes what the skill contains.

**B2. Whether "the author says the design is finished" is the sign-off, and what `close-out` does when it loads early.**
Passage 1: *"The `close-out` skill runs step 8, and is loaded when the author says the design is
finished or asks to sign it off."* Passage 2: *"**Signing off.** The author declares the design
finished, which allows it to be cut into slices."* Step 8 (§3) runs *"in this order"*: item 1 runs the
checks and may re-run the ledger review, item 2 proposes the sort, and *"3. The author signs off, which
also approves the sort."* The front matter says *finished* when *"the author saying so"* *"means what
this file calls signing off."*
- If saying the design is finished *is* the sign-off, the sign-off happens before items 1 and 2. That
  breaks step 8's order, and the author approves a sort that has not yet been proposed.
- If it is only the trigger that loads the skill, the passage does not say what `close-out` does when
  it loads while steps 5–7 are incomplete: for example during writing, or before the document and seam
  reviews have run. Does it refuse, run the missing steps, or send the work back? Step 8 item 1 checks
  only the checklist and the ledger review, not steps 5 or 7.
I would have assumed the trigger reading, and that `close-out` refuses until step 7 has ended. Nothing
in the text says so.

## 2. AMBIGUOUS

**A1. "`METHOD.md`'s line naming the skills".**
Passage 1: *"Two skills carry it, and `METHOD.md`'s line naming the skills names both."* `METHOD.md`
names skills in two places: the "Which skill runs it" column of *When each review fires*, and the
sentence *"Six more skills hold the rest, loaded at the moment each is wanted: `slice` … `handoff`"*.
Reading 1: the sentence beginning "Six more skills", which then needs its count changed from "Six".
Reading 2: some new line that does not yet exist. It is also unclear whether this describes
`METHOD.md` as it is now, which names neither skill, or as it must become. I would have taken the
"Six more skills" sentence, and assumed the count goes up to eight. The passage gives no count.

**A2. Moving a document back on abandonment: which version, whether it is conditional, and which cases are covered.**
Passage 2: *"the agent offers one clean-up: deleting the elephant's directory, drafts included, after
first moving back to its old path any document the elephant moved into it (§7)."*
- Version: the moved document has been edited in place by then (§7: *"each section of the successor
  replaces its old counterpart there as it is written"*). Is it moved back as edited, or as committed?
  The passage does not say. §7 says *"as committed when it moved"*, but only for replacement. A
  document moved in to be **extended or changed** (§6: *"moves into the new elephant's directory as a
  `draft` when the author rules to extend or change it"*) is also covered by "any document the elephant
  moved into it", and for that case no version is given.
- Uncommitted move: if the move was never committed, "as committed when it moved" names nothing. §7
  handles this for the retirement review (*"or as last committed before the move where the move has not
  been committed"*), but not for the move back.
- Conditional or not: Reading 1: moving back is part of the clean-up being offered, so an author who
  declines the clean-up leaves the document in the directory. Reading 2: moving back always happens
  first. §7 (*"before the agent offers the clean-up"*) supports reading 2. The passage's sentence
  supports either.
- Edited copy: under reading 2, it is not said whether the in-place-edited version stays in the
  directory as a "kept draft" that the author may keep.
- Voided slices: §6 says that moving a `finalised` document in voids its slices, *"deleted from the
  list of slices, with any plan written for them"*. The move back does not say whether they are
  restored, or what status the restored document carries. It went in marked `draft`, and the committed
  copy says `finalised`.
I would have taken: always move back, as last committed before the move, status as committed, slices
not restored. All four parts are guesses.

**A3. "Every question … has one form", with exceptions, and which exchanges count as a question.**
Passage 2: *"Every question put to the author, at any step, has one form. … offers two or more
candidate answers, with the one the agent recommends first and marked as such."* Later in the same
paragraph: *"names one of the two questions that carry no recommendation"*. Reading 1: the form is
universal, and those two questions are exempt only from the recommendation. Reading 2: those two
questions are outside the form altogether, and need not carry the one-sentence issue statement or the
background either. I would have taken reading 1.
It is also unclear whether these must take the form:
- the agent's *"follow-up questions about what the author meant"* during writing (§3 step 5). These
  are clarifications, where candidate answers and a recommended answer about the author's own meaning
  are odd;
- the agent's proposal to end the interview (*"The agent proposes ending it, and the author agrees or
  refuses"*);
- each review finding put to the author.
I would have applied the form to all three.

**A4. "for a stated reason": stated by whom.**
Passage 2: *"**immaterial** — the finding is right, but it is not acted on, for a stated reason;
**rejected** — the finding is wrong, for a stated reason."*
Reading 1: the author must state the reason.
Reading 2: the agent's recommendation carries a reason, and the author's bare "rejected" adopts it.
The passage also does not say what happens when the author rules "immaterial" with no reason. Is the
question put again, or is the ruling incomplete? I would have taken reading 2. "Answering in prose" or
"choosing an option" would then both satisfy it.

**A5. "The agent need not handle a change of topic in that conversation".**
Passage 2: *"The agent need not handle a change of topic in that conversation: the author works
elsewhere, and returns to the elephant in the same conversation or through a handoff when they are
ready."*
- "need not handle": Reading 1, the agent may do the other work in the same conversation. Reading 2,
  the agent must not, and the other work happens "elsewhere", which means another session.
  "returns to the elephant in the same conversation" suggests the other work did happen in that
  conversation, which supports reading 1.
- "that conversation": the antecedent is unclear. It might be the conversation in which the author
  stopped replying, or the one in which a deferral was made.
- Unstated: whether work on the other topic may touch the elephant's drafts or working files.
I would have taken reading 1 and assumed the other work leaves the elephant's directory alone.

**A6. Deferral: who decides what "depends on" the answer, and whether a ruling at one of the four points can be deferred.**
Passage 2: *"A deferral the author volunteers holds up only the work that depends on the deferred
answer, and the question waits: on the checklist when it is deferred in the interview (step 2), and
otherwise in the handoff, as any unruled finding does."*
- The passage does not say who judges which work depends on the deferred answer, or whether the author
  is told what is being held up.
- "otherwise in the handoff": a handoff exists only when a session closes. Before that, nothing records
  where an outside-the-interview deferral is waiting.
- A deferral of a ruling at one of the four points is not addressed: deferring the decision to end the
  interview, or a ruling on a review finding. For the interview, does the interview continue or pause?
  For a section-review finding, step 5 says the next section does not start, so "only the work that
  depends" is in practice everything that comes after.
I would have assumed that the agent judges what depends, and that deferring the end of the interview
means the interview continues.

**A7. "If the author stops replying … nothing happens to it", against the session ending anyway.**
Passage 2: *"If the author stops replying, the elephant stays exactly where it is and nothing happens to
it."* A session can still end, through the `handoff` skill or compaction. §3 step 2 says that when a
session is handed off during the interview, *"the ending session dispatches the interview review …
before it closes"*. Reading 1: "nothing happens" means no ruling is assumed, and the agent still writes
a handoff and dispatches the review. Reading 2: the agent takes no action of any kind, including those.
I would have taken reading 1.

**A8. PILOT.md: "how to abandon one" against "says nothing of the working files".**
Passage 1: *"… and how to abandon one. It says nothing of the working files or of how the reviews are
run."* Passage 2 makes abandonment a choice about files: *"The author may accept it or keep any of its
files … If the author keeps any file, the ledger is kept with it"*. The ledger is a working file (§4).
Reading 1: PILOT explains abandonment without naming the files. The author is then offered a choice
about files they were never told about. Reading 2: PILOT names the files only as part of
abandonment. That breaks "says nothing of the working files". I would have taken reading 2.

**A9. Is "what else the author wants to discuss" part of the ruling point?**
Passage 2: *"**Ending the interview**, including the question of what else the author wants to discuss
(step 2)."* Combined with *"'You decide' is a legal answer to any question except … a ruling at any of
the four points"*. Reading 1: "you decide" to the what-else question is illegal, and the question is put
again. Reading 2: the point is only the agree/refuse ruling on the proposal to end, and the what-else
question is a separate question. It is also unclear whether choosing an area counts as a "ruling" at
all. I would have taken reading 1.

**A10. Which parts of a review report count as findings.**
Passage 2: *"Each finding is ruled on separately, and gets one of three outcomes"*. Every review prompt
has a category for concerns the reviewer could not ground, and a closing account of what it understood
(§5). The ledger and retirement reports have *"found"* entries, which §5 and §7 say are *"not ruled
on"*. The passage does not say whether ungrounded concerns are findings to rule on. I would have ruled
on them, but that is a guess.

**A11. "where the question is put again".**
Passage 2: *"'You decide' is a legal answer to any question except three kinds, where the question is
put again"*. The passage does not say whether the question is re-put with the same wording or with an
explanation that delegation is not available. Nor does it say what happens when the author answers
"you decide" a second time. Under "No ruling has a default", presumably the elephant waits forever. I
would have re-put it with a one-line explanation.

## 3. UNDEFINED TERMS

**U1. "points in the process": what makes a ruling one.**
Passage 1: *"four of those rulings are points in the process, listed below."* Passage 2 lists four:
starting, ending the interview, each review finding, signing off. Other rulings are not among them: the
step-1 ruling on each document found (replace/extend/change/leave), the *nothing covers this* ruling,
the step-4 agreement on documents and sections, the elephant's name, and new-ground questions. No
criterion separates the two groups, yet the term is relied on precisely. "You decide" is illegal at
these points. PILOT is to describe *"the four points where it stops for the author's ruling"*, which
reads as if these were the only places the agent stops, while passage 1 says *"the author rules on
every question it puts to them"*. A reader cannot classify a new ruling, such as one added by a later
change, as a point or not.

**U2. "finished" (of the design).**
Passage 1: *"loaded when the author says the design is finished"*. Passage 2: *"The author declares the
design finished"*. Neither passage defines it. The front matter equates *finished* with signing off,
which is what produces B2.

**U3. "the proposal (§1)" as something a skill "runs".**
Passage 1: *"The `elephant` skill runs the proposal (§1)"*. §1 describes the agent saying whether a
change needs an elephant. It defines no procedure that a skill could "run", and it does not say whether
"the proposal" covers proposing *against* an elephant. See B1.

## 4. UNVERIFIABLE CLAIMS

**V1. The citation of §9 resolves to nothing.**
Passage 1: *"the start of a change and the end of a design, sessions later, are asked for in words that
have nothing in common (§9)."* The `ELEPHANT.md` I was given ends at §7. There is no §9, so the claim,
and the premise *"a skill is loaded when a request matches its description"*, cannot be checked. This
is the stated reason for having two skills.

**V2.** Passage 2: *"A question that arrives with its options and a recommendation costs the author less
to answer than one that does not."* No source or measurement is given, and nothing would check it.

**V3.** Passage 2: *"carries enough background to be understood by somebody who has not seen the
earlier questions"*. No test is given for "enough", and no reader is defined. An agent writing the
question cannot check its own question against this.

**V4.** Passage 2: *"names one of the two questions that carry no recommendation; the other is
close-out's question about reading the finished document"*. This claims exactly two such questions in
the whole process. I could not confirm it is complete. §7's question *"whether each one's reason still
holds"*, about rejected alternatives, does not say whether it carries a recommendation, and neither
does the agent's proposal to end the interview.

## 5. CONTRADICTIONS

**C1. The three outcomes against `METHOD.md`'s three dispositions: names, order, and the meaning of "accepted".**
Passage 2: *"**accepted** — the finding is right, and it is acted on; **immaterial** — the finding is
right, but it is not acted on, for a stated reason; **rejected** — the finding is wrong … These are the
three outcomes `METHOD.md` gives every finding, under these names and in this order"*.
`METHOD.md`, *The three dispositions*: *"**Fixed** | the finding is right and the document changes"*,
*"**Accepted with a reason** | the finding is right and the document stays as it is anyway"*,
*"**Rejected with a reason**"*; and *What a review is worth*: *"fixed, accepted with a reason, or
rejected with a reason"*.
The names differ. The two "accepted"s also have opposite meanings. In `METHOD.md` "accepted" means
*not* acted on, which the passage calls "immaterial". In the passage, "accepted" means acted on, which
`METHOD.md` calls "Fixed". If the passage describes `METHOD.md` as it must become, it does not say so.
As written, it asserts something about `METHOD.md` that is false today.

**C2. The quoted overriding rule against the rule as written.**
Passage 2: *"its overriding rule names none of them: 'A finding is a claim for the **author** to rule on
— never an instruction to whoever ran the review.'"*
`METHOD.md`, line 15: *"A finding is a claim for the **author** to accept, reject or act on — never an
instruction to whoever ran the review."*
The quotation does not match the text it quotes. The claim "names none of them" is also false of the
real rule, which names "accept" and "reject". Under the passage's own scheme, "accept" is one of the
three names, and it carries the opposite meaning from C1. The passage does not mark the quotation as
wording to be introduced.

**C3. Abandoning "at any point" during close-out.**
Passage 2: *"The author may abandon an elephant at any point"*, then *"Nothing the design says has been
written into a live document (§1)"* and *"Nothing is signed off"*.
§3 step 8: *"3. The author signs off … 4. The sort is carried out: each draft leaves the elephant's
directory, marked finalised, for its final path"*, and *"It ends when every draft has left the
elephant's directory, the author has signed off, and the working files are deleted."*
§1 defines a live document as *"one outside every elephant's directory whose status is not `draft`"*.
Between item 3 and the end of item 4 the elephant is still running, so it can be abandoned. But the
author has already signed off, and drafts already moved out are live documents. If the author abandons
at that point, both "Nothing is signed off" and "Nothing the design says has been written into a live
document" are false.

## 7. CONCERNS YOU COULD NOT GROUND

**G1.** Abandoning an elephant that moved `CORRIDORS.md` or `FORMATIONS.md` into its directory looks as
if it would break the status check. §6 says each document's exemption from the rule against `draft` *"is
deleted when that document's design resumes in an elephant and the document moves into its directory"*.
The clean-up in passage 2 moves the document back to the root, still `draft`, with its exemption
already gone. I can point at both texts, but I cannot tell whether the exemption is deleted at the move
or at close-out. So I cannot say for certain that the two collide.

**G2.** The `elephant` skill is to run "abandoning an elephant", but an author can ask to abandon in
words unrelated to the skill's description, and can do so during step 8, when `close-out` is the skill
loaded. This is the same mismatch the passage gives as the reason for splitting the skills. No text says
which skill handles abandonment requested during close-out. I could not ground more than that.

**G3.** *"No ruling has a default or a time limit"* and *"An elephant the author has stopped replying to
has not been abandoned"*, combined with §7's rules, can block other work. Under §7 a `draft` in another
running elephant's directory is *"offered no option until that elephant ends"*, and an elephant facing
an `implementation ongoing` document *"waits"*. So a forgotten elephant could block other elephants
indefinitely, and nothing distinguishes forgotten from paused. I cannot point at text that makes this
a defect, only at the combination.

**G4.** "You decide" is legal on the clean-up offer, which deletes drafts. It is also legal on step 4's
choice between answering and omitting an open checklist item. Both let the agent destroy work, or drop a
topic, on delegation. The passage carves out only three kinds of question and does not consider these.
I could not ground that this is wrong rather than intended.

## 8. WHAT YOU WOULD BUILD

An elephant is a long-running, multi-session procedure in which an agent questions the author about a
change, then writes the resulting design one section at a time into drafts in a private directory,
with fresh reviewers checking each stage. It runs eight ordered steps: search, interview, interview
review, decisions, writing, ledger review, document and seam reviews, close-out. Steps can be revisited
when the interview is reopened or the author sends work back. Two skills carry it: `elephant` for the
proposal through step 7 and for abandonment, and `close-out` for step 8. The author's guide, `PILOT.md`,
describes it from the author's side. The author controls four fixed points (opting in, ending the
interview, ruling on every review finding as accepted, immaterial or rejected, and signing off), and
rules on every other question the agent puts. Every question has a fixed shape: issue and why it matters
in one sentence, standalone background, and two or more options with the agent's pick first. Nothing is
ever decided by default or timeout, and "you decide" is refused only at the four points, on a *nothing
covers this* claim, and on the read-the-document question. The author can abandon at any time; the agent
then moves back any document it pulled in and offers to delete the directory, and any kept files are
marked abandoned with the ledger kept alongside.
